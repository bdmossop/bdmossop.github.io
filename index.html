Building AI systems that learn and reason about causation

I develop causal AI systems that learn structure, model how systems change, and reason about the consequences of interventions under uncertainty. My work brings together causal discovery, forecasting, Bayesian inference, simulation, and agent-based modelling, with a growing focus on causal world models and physical AI.

Current research direction

Much of my current work focuses on causal AI, particularly the intersection of causal discovery, forecasting, simulation, and decision-making.

I am especially interested in how these ideas can contribute to world models and physical AI. Many AI systems can be viewed as learning a policy

\[\begin{equation} \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t), \end{equation}\]

which determines what action an agent should take in its current state. World models go a step further by learning how the environment evolves under those actions,

\[\begin{equation} T_\phi(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t). \end{equation}\]

I am particularly interested in giving these learned world models an explicitly causal representation,

\[\begin{equation} T_\phi(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, \operatorname{do}(a_t)), \end{equation}\]

where actions are treated as interventions on the environment. The broader goal is to develop AI systems that can reason about possible consequences before acting, while remaining robust, interpretable, and uncertainty-aware.
